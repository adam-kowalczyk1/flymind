# Competitive / state-of-the-art landscape: connectome-driven spiking control of robots and simulated bodies (as of 3 Nov 2026)

Scope note: research done 2026-11-03 with web search + page fetches. "Verified" = I read the primary page (paper abstract, lab page, repo README). "Unverified" = secondary/aggregator or social-media claim. Several 2026 hobby repos carry no dates on their pages; I flag this. No source found that describes an SO-101/SO-100/LeRobot arm driven by a fly connectome, but absence in search is not proof of absence (see Gaps).

## 1. Foundations: FlyWire connectome, Shiu et al. LIF whole-brain model, visual-system model

### Takeaway
The data (FlyWire, 2024; MaleCNS, Sept 2026) and the standard whole-brain LIF model (Shiu et al., Nature 2024) are public and have become a commodity: many hobbyists now run them. The scientific papers themselves are open-loop/in-silico; none involves a robot.

### Cited Findings
- Dorkenwald et al., "Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain", Nature 634:124-138, published 2 Oct 2024: 139,255 neurons, ~50 million chemical synapses, adult female Drosophila, predicted neurotransmitters, >8,000 cell types; lead paper of a nine-paper package. — [Princeton Pure record / summary](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/neuronal-wiring-diagram-of-an-adult-brain/), [NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/complete-wiring-map-adult-fruit-fly-brain)
- Shiu et al., "A Drosophila computational brain model reveals sensorimotor processing", Nature 634:210-219, 3 Oct 2024 (DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9): leaky integrate-and-fire model of the whole brain from connectivity + neurotransmitter identity; one free parameter (global synaptic weight scale); predicted which gustatory neurons respond to tastes / are needed for feeding initiation, and the antennal grooming circuit activation from mechanosensory stimulation. Counts quoted vary by source (~127k proofread neurons, ~50M synapses). The paper is a computational/in-silico model, no robot. — [Princeton Pure](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/a-drosophila-computational-brain-model-reveals-sensorimotor-proce/), [IDEAS/RePEc entry](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v634y2024i8032d10.1038_s41586-024-07763-9.html)
- Open-source reimplementation of the Shiu model across backends (Brian2, Brian2CUDA, PyTorch, NEST GPU, GeNN) by Eon Systems PBC's public repo; GPL-2.0-or-later; mentions no robot or neuromorphic chip implementation on its page. The repo attributes "91% prediction accuracy vs experimental fly data" to the Nature paper. — [eonsystemspbc/fly-brain](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain)
- Lappalainen et al., "Connectome-constrained networks predict neural activity across the fly visual system", Nature 634:1132-1140 (2024): network of 64 optic-lobe cell types with connectome-fixed wiring, unknown single-neuron/synapse parameters tuned by deep learning to detect motion; predictions agree with 26 studies. This is a trained (deep-learning) rate-style model, not a spiking whole-brain model, and not embodied. — [Max Planck IS record](https://is.mpg.de/publications/lappalainennature24), [Uni Tübingen press release](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/university/news-and-publications/press-release/researchers-combine-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence-and-the-wiring-diagram-of-a-brain-to-predict-brain-cell-activity/)
- MaleCNS v1.0 (male adult fly full CNS incl. nerve cord): published in Cell on 3 Sept 2026 (Berg et al., doi 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015); ~166,700 neurons, ~11,700 types, ~125M synapses per Google Research; CC-BY; earlier v0.9 release Oct 2025. Coverage of viral demos (Minecraft, Doom) is second-hand and unverified. — [Live Science summary](https://livescience.com/health/neuroscience/new-map-of-a-male-fly-central-nervous-system-includes-all-166-000-neurons-and-enables-direct-comparisons-to-female-fly-brain), [Janelia page](https://www.janelia.org/node/70079)
- Sandia (Wang, Theilman, Rothganger, Severa, Vineyard, Aimone), arXiv 2508.16792, 22 Aug 2025: full FlyWire connectome (~140k neurons, 50M synapses) mapped onto 12 Intel Loihi 2 chips; statistically validated against reference simulations; "orders of magnitude" faster than conventional simulation. Abstract mentions no robot or closed-loop test. — [arXiv 2508.16792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16792)
- Bates et al. brain-and-cord connectome analysis ("Distributed control circuits across a brain-and-cord connectome") describes the fly's architecture as distributed/parallel/embodied (secondary summary only). — [PMC12324551](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12324551/)

### Inferences
- Anyone claiming novelty merely for "running the FlyWire/MaleCNS connectome as a spiking LIF network" is not novel; it is a reproduced public baseline (Eon repo, many GitHub repos).
- The cited science of "sign-of-synapse from predicted neurotransmitter" is itself uncertain (Eon and Fly Brain Bridge both flag predicted signs as a limitation, see below), which matters for any claim that behaviour "comes from the wiring".

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve the Shiu 2024 full text; numeric details (e.g., exact accuracy figures) rely on abstracts/secondary pages.

## 2. Simulated fly bodies: NeuroMechFly v2 / flygym (EPFL), flybody (DeepMind + Janelia), connectome-driven control in simulation

### Takeaway
The simulated-body side is well developed: NeuroMechFly v2 (EPFL) and flybody (Janelia/DeepMind) are the standard bodies; Eon Systems (Mar 2026) and many GitHub projects couple the Shiu-type connectome LIF brain to them. All of it is simulation.

### Cited Findings
- NeuroMechFly v2: Wang-Chen et al., Nature Methods 21(12), online 12 Nov 2024 (DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02497-y), Ramdya lab EPFL. Adds vision, olfaction, ascending motor feedback, complex terrain, leg adhesion; software = FlyGym (Python, MuJoCo); includes a connectome-constrained visual-system network for fly-following. — [EPFL news](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/simulating-how-fruit-flies-see-smell-and-navigat-7), [FlyGym on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/flygym), [author postprint](https://epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf)
- flybody: Vaxenburg et al., "Whole-body physics simulation of fruit fly locomotion", Nature 643:1312-1320, 31 Jul 2025 (DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09029-4), HHMI Janelia + Google DeepMind. MuJoCo model, 67 body parts / 66 joints / 102 DoF, walking + flight, controlled by a neural network trained with deep RL on real fly behaviour video (NOT a connectome); code at github.com/TuragaLab/flybody; authors list ventral-nerve-cord connectome-driven control as a possible future direction. — [Janelia news](https://janelia.org/news/artificial-intelligence-brings-a-virtual-fly-to-life), [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v643y2025i8074d10.1038_s41586-025-09029-4.html)
- Eon Systems "virtual embodied fly" (published 10 Mar 2026 on Eon's site; press 7-10 Mar): Shiu et al. LIF central-brain model (~140k neurons, ~50M synapses) + Lappalainen connectome-constrained vision + NeuroMechFly v2 (87 joints, MuJoCo); brain-body sync every 15 ms; small set of descending neurons (DNa01/DNa02 steering, oDN1 forward velocity, MN9 feeding, antennal DNs grooming) as control interface. Behaviours: antennal grooming, feeding, foraging toward a taste cue; looming escape modelled in brain but not implemented in the body. Everything simulated. Eon's own stated limits (quoted from their post): "many of the mappings between brain and body were chosen by hand rather than derived from the connectome"; vision outputs are "somewhat 'decorative'" and don't substantially influence behaviour; "we have not yet validated the model's internal dynamics against known biological signatures"; "The fly body is not currently driven by the full downstream motor hierarchy"; walking controllers are imitation-trained NeuroMechFly controllers; 15 ms step may be too slow. Described as "a research platform and a demonstration platform". — [Eon: How the Eon Team Produced a Virtual Embodied Fly](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation)
- Press reaction to Eon: The Decoder (10 Mar 2026) notes the brain model is the 2024 Shiu model, and that the novel part (the body coupling) was not yet released at the time, so not independently checkable; no independent expert quoted. Other outlets use stronger "first embodied whole-brain emulation / upload" language that Eon's own text does not support. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/startup-claims-first-full-brain-emulation-of-a-fruit-fly-in-a-simulated-body/), [critique: "this is not a fly uploaded to a computer"](https://news.ainauten.com/markdown/this-is-not-a-fly-uploaded-to-a-computer)
- Jin, Zhu, Zhang, Sui, "Whole-Brain Connectomic Graph Model Enables Whole-Body Locomotion Control in Fruit Fly", arXiv 2602.17997 (20 Feb 2026, v3 14 Jun 2026): adult fly connectome used as a graph-structured controller of a biomechanical fly, trained with deep RL; simulation only; reports sample-efficiency gains over baselines. Graph neural controller, not a spiking model. — [arXiv 2602.17997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997)
- Zhang, Lin, Lu, "FlyCNS", arXiv 2609.28816 (23 Sep 2026): fly brain-and-nerve-cord connectome-derived structural measure weakly guides communication allocation across limbs of a simulated Unitree Go1 quadruped; RL-trained; simulation; spiking not stated. — [arXiv 2609.28816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28816)
- Guan et al., "Humanoid Locomotion with a Fly-Inspired Recurrent Controller", arXiv 2609.27001 (22 Sep 2026): 3,609 continuous neural states driving simulated Unitree G1; simulation study; connectome dataset not named, probably non-spiking. — [arXiv 2609.27001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27001)
- Hobby/independent GitHub simulations (all simulation, undated pages with 2026 footers; claims are the authors' own, not peer-reviewed):
  - Froststar16/connectome-control: FlyWire v783 LIF (Brian2 + PyTorch) closing the loop on NeuroMechFly v2 for looming escape; author's caveats: giant fibre barely fires on author's connectome, most closed-loop results use 3 seeds. — [repo](https://github.com/Froststar16/connectome-control)
  - erojasoficial-byte/fly-brain: 138,639-neuron LIF on NeuroMechFly v2 with Hebbian plasticity; claims "first embodied whole-brain spiking simulation"; behaviour-mode selection is a hand-designed bridge; "consciousness" metrics are the author's own proxies; Zenodo preprint, not peer-reviewed. — [repo](https://github.com/erojasoficial-byte/fly-brain)
  - TheJustas228/connectocopter (simulated FPV quadcopter-rover, closed loop, GPU LIF) — [repo](https://github.com/TheJustas228/connectocopter); huang12chen/flywire-brain-drone (escape circuit piloting simulated drone) — [repo](https://github.com/huang12chen/flywire-brain-drone); sabishii-bit/neurofly-kit (MaleCNS wrapper; MuJoCo flybody + PC I/O; no physical robot) — [repo](https://github.com/sabishii-bit/neurofly-kit); ekorusd/flywire-snn-brain (game NPCs) — [repo](https://github.com/ekorusd/flywire-snn-brain); SingularityEdge FlyDrone-ESN (connectome as fixed echo-state reservoir with trained ridge readout, PyBullet drone sim) — [HF](https://huggingface.co/SingularityEdge/FlyDrone-ESN)
- Embodied-simulation baseline list: independent secondary overview asserts NeuroMechFly v2 and flybody "have been used with connectome-derived circuits for closed-loop walking and obstacle avoidance" and cites Sandia/Loihi (blog, low reliability). — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/drosophila-whole-brain-connectome-flywire)

### Inferences
- "Connectome LIF brain closes a loop with a simulated MuJoCo fly" is now done by a funded startup plus multiple individuals; it is not a differentiator.
- Common pattern across Eon and the hobby repos: sensory encoding and the descending-neuron-to-motor decoder are hand-designed. The honest scientific claim is "wiring plus a designed interface," not "wiring alone produces behaviour." This is exactly the interface a student SO-101 project would also have to design.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether Ramdya lab has published its own spiking whole-brain-in-NeuroMechFly work beyond the visual-system module; search did not surface one.
- Eon's code for the body coupling was reported unreleased as of Mar 2026 (The Decoder); I did not check whether it was released later.

## 3. Physical robots driven by a fly/insect connectome (the key question)

### Takeaway
A physical-hardware fly-connectome robot is at most at hobbyist, partly-tested stage as of Nov 2026: the closest are "Fly Brain Bridge" (Sesame quadruped, MaleCNS LIF) and FlyDrones (Tello/Crazyflie adapters), and in both the real-hardware loop is explicitly not yet demonstrated by the authors' own READMEs. I found no robot arm, no SO-100/SO-101/LeRobot project, and no peer-reviewed physical fly-connectome robot.

### Cited Findings
- Fly Brain Bridge (Dorian Todd, 2026, status "released"): MaleCNS v1.0 (166,700 neurons, ~25.6M filtered connections) LIF brain on a desktop PC (32 threads) controlling a Sesame ESP32 quadruped over WiFi HTTP JSON; stimuli injected into identified sensory neurons; command neurons (MN9, Giant Fiber, aDN1/2, MDN, DNa01/02, P9/BDN2/oDN1) mapped to behaviours by a hand-designed scorer; joint mode uses 36 leg motor-neuron pools into a learned 485-parameter CPG. Author states: joint-streaming firmware, brain OLED, startle pose, webcam tracking "built and tested in software only"; gait interface trained/tested only in MuJoCo; first real-hardware gait test (robot held in the air) still planned; no physical sensor feedback from robot into the brain described, i.e. hardware path is effectively open-loop; command-to-behaviour mapping designed by the author; point neurons, predicted neurotransmitter signs. — [doriantodd.com/projects/fly-brain-bridge](https://www.doriantodd.com/projects/fly-brain-bridge/)
- FlyDrones (SpikeCalls): camera -> per-cell optic flow -> MaleCNS spiking network (166k neurons) -> descending neurons (DNg02, DNp01) -> stick commands for a drone; drone's own flight controller stabilises and an external safety governor has final say. README: Tello/Crazyflie/MAVLink/ESP32 adapters "written against the official SDKs but have not been flight-tested by us yet"; "first real Tello flight with full MaleCNS brain" is an unchecked roadmap item; browser demo uses an 850-neuron hand-wired stand-in ("MiniFly"), not the real connectome; full brain ran at ~0.62x real time on a 2-vCPU cloud VM; sensory mapping and readout engineered by the authors. — [FlyDrones README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SpikeCalls/FlyDrones/HEAD/README.md), [guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SpikeCalls/FlyDrones/HEAD/docs/GUIDE.md)
- An X post (AGTP, undated in my retrieval) asserts that "the fruit fly connectome just started piloting drones and quadruped robots" and cites MaleCNS; I could not verify it against any primary source and it appears to be a summary of the two hobby projects above. — [X post](https://x.com/AGTPinsights/status/2100169978301321570) (unverified)
- Search for connectome + robot arm / servo / manipulator found nothing: GitHub search results for FlyWire spiking projects listed none driving an arm or servos (all are simulated bodies, drones, games, or analysis). — [Froststar16/connectome-control](https://github.com/Froststar16/connectome-control), [connectocopter](https://github.com/TheJustas228/connectocopter), [BioGrid](https://github.com/dorywoof/BioGrid), [flywire-brain-sim](https://github.com/emrezorlu1239/flywire-brain-sim) (listing evidence only)
- SO-101/LeRobot search: LeRobot lists SO-101 as supported hardware; separate repos exist for fly-connectome SNNs (flynet, lofly, FlyDrones), but nothing combining them. — [huggingface/lerobot](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot), [Zikithezikit/flynet](https://github.com/Zikithezikit/flynet), [jeffory/lofly](https://github.com/jeffory/lofly)
- Spiking/neuromorphic control of robot arms generally (not connectome-based, not fly): Traub, Legenstein, Otte recurrent SNN control of up to 75-DoF trunk-like arms (simulation) — [arXiv 2104.04064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04064); closed-loop neuromorphic control of an event-based ED-Scorbot arm on DYNAP-SE2 — [arXiv 2501.17172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17172v1); SNN accelerator (ReckOn on FPGA) arm control — [arXiv 2405.12849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12849); spiking cerebellar model + robot arm in vision-based manipulation — [arXiv 2102.01966](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01966); WTA/CPG spiking arm control — [arXiv 2202.07064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.07064)
- Student precedent (not connectome-based): Mihir Garimella (high school, 2014 Google Science Fair finalist) built a fruit-fly-inspired escape quadrotor using IR sensors, not a connectome. — [Post-Gazette, 2014](https://www.post-gazette.com/news/science/2014/07/29/Local-student-a-finalist-in-Google-Science-Fair-for-his-flying-robot-inspired-by-fruit-fly/stories/201407220008). I found no report of a high-school student driving a robot with a fly connectome. A commentary says that once code is open the fly brain "will be connected to ... even real robot bodies" (speculation) — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/janelia-google-male-fly-connectome-goes-viral-on-minecraft-demo).
- C. elegans precedent (physical robots): Timothy Busbice (OpenWorm co-founder), 2014: 302-neuron C. elegans connectome run as software (integrate-and-fire neurons as objects, UDP packets) with a LEGO Mindstorms robot (sonar = nose, motors = body muscles); touch/nose/food sensors produced stop/forward/back behaviour without explicit programming; later moved to Raspberry Pi; critics noted it drops the analog calcium-based biology and gap-junction/chemical distinctions. A 2018 JHU APL "Worminator" paper describes a platform connecting the connectome to a TurtleBot via ROS. — [Elektor](https://elektormagazine.com/articles/ai-a-worm-brain-powering-a-lego-robot), [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/scientists-put-worm-brain-in-lego-robot-openworm-connectome), [CCN 2018 proceedings](https://2018.ccneuro.org/proceedings/1149.pdf)
- Related: biomimetic physical fruit-fly-scale legged robot (not connectome-driven), Bioinspiration & Biomimetics 2024. — [IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-3190/ad80ec)

### Inferences
- A physical fly-connectome-driven robot DOES already exist in nascent form: Fly Brain Bridge targets a real quadruped and FlyDrones targets real drones, with open-source code in 2026. Anyone presenting "first physical robot controlled by the fly connectome" would be factually challengeable. C. elegans-connectome robots date back to 2014, so "connectome in hardware" per se is not new either.
- What I could NOT find, and what therefore is possibly new: (a) a manipulator arm; (b) SO-101/LeRobot specifically; (c) a demonstrated, validated, real-hardware closed loop with servo/encoder feedback into the connectome network and a documented control-theoretic test (tracking error, perturbation response); (d) controls such as degree-preserving rewired connectome vs real wiring on physical hardware. Both existing physical projects state their hardware loop is untested; so a student demonstration of actual measured closed-loop on real hardware with publicly reported ablations would exceed the current state of those projects. This depends on the student actually achieving and rigorously testing it.
- Caveat on defensibility: if FlyDrones/Fly Brain Bridge run their real-hardware tests soon, the "first physical" angle shrinks further; novelty should be framed narrowly (arm + low-cost reproducible kit + rigorous controls), not "first physical".

### Gaps
- Search tools are US-only and weak on YouTube/Hackaday/Reddit/Discord content; I could not check YouTube or Hackaday directly. A hobbyist SO-101 + fly brain video may exist without being indexed.
- The Hugging Face repo "hwihwalab/malecns-connectome-robotics-2026" appeared in search but returned HTTP 401; contents unknown (title suggests a connectome-robotics resource). — [HF link](https://huggingface.co/hwihwalab/malecns-connectome-robotics-2026) (not read)
- I did not verify whether Fly Brain Bridge or FlyDrones have since reported real-hardware tests (their pages carry no dates).
- No peer-reviewed physical fly-connectome robot found; I did not search every 2026 conference (ICRA/IROS/CoRL) proceedings.

## 4. Eon Systems (company, funding, next steps)

### Takeaway
Eon Systems PBC is the only funded entity pursuing fly brain emulation as a product-like effort; $3.5M seed (Dec 2025), mouse-brain target in ~2 years, no physical robot announced as of the evidence found.

### Cited Findings
- Seed: $3.5M, led by Protocol Labs, with Larry Page's family office reported by one listing; announced around 30 Dec 2025 (Untapped Ventures post). Stated goals: complete mouse brain emulation "within two years," longer-term disease and AI-model applications. — [Untapped Ventures](https://untappedventures.substack.com/p/untapped-ventures-invests-in-eon), [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/eon-systems/funding-rounds/seed-2025-12)
- Co-founder Alex Wissner-Gross; team on the fly post: Scott Harris, Aarav Sinha, Viktor Toth, Alexis Pomares, Philip Shiu (first author of the 2024 Shiu model). — [Eon post](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/startup-claims-first-full-brain-emulation-of-a-fruit-fly-in-a-simulated-body/)
- Experts reported as skeptical on the mouse timeline (a decade or more); a July 2026 piece quotes Philip Shiu saying segmentation/proofreading is the core challenge for the mouse. — [Seedtable/search summary pages](https://seedtable.com/companies/eon-systems), [Startbase](https://www.startbase.com/news/startup-eon-systems-demonstriert-erste-multi-verhaltensfaehige-gehirn-emulation/) (secondary, low-to-medium reliability)
- Eon's own post describes no physical hardware or wet-lab work. — [Eon post](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation)

### Inferences
- Eon is an adjacent competitor for the narrative "connectome emulation" but not for low-cost physical manipulation; it could add a physical body, but nothing found suggests that.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of newer Eon funding or of a physical-robot effort after March 2026; searches returned mostly March-April 2026 coverage.

## 5. "Biological intelligence" and insect-inspired companies (funding)

### Takeaway
These are different technologies (living neurons or insect-inspired algorithms, not connectome-derived SNNs) and are context for investors, not direct competitors. Funding figures below are at best moderate-reliability; several aggregators conflict.

### Cited Findings
- Cortical Labs (Melbourne): DishBrain (~800,000 human neurons learning Pong) reported in Neuron, Dec 2022; CL1 "code-deployable biological computer" announced Mar 2025 at $35,000, shipping summer 2025; Cortical Cloud wetware-as-a-service at $300/week; funding: ~$1.56M early, $10M in Apr 2025 led by Horizons Ventures (with Blackbird and In-Q-Tel), AUD 245k government grant in 2026; Aug 2026 DayOne prototype Singapore biological data centre with 20 CL1 units. Wikipedia carries a source-reliability warning. — [Wikipedia: Cortical Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortical_Labs), [IT Brief on grant](https://itbrief.com.au/story/cortical-labs-cl1-commercial-milestone-after-aud-245k-grant)
- Conflicting Cortical Labs totals: CB Insights ~ $11M, others $10.7M-$12.5M; an earlier US$10M round reported in Apr 2023 and a "$15M Horizon Ventures-led" report from 2023 exist in business press, which conflicts with Wikipedia's Apr 2025 $10M date; unresolved. A Gobi Partners investment in Cortical Labs was reported March 2026 without an amount in the retrieved text. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/cortical-labs), [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/biotech-cortical-labs-closes--15m-oversubscribed-funding-round-led-by-horizon-ventures.html), [TechNode](https://technode.global/2026/03/04/malaysia-based-gobi-partners-invests-in-cortical-labs-expands-biological-computing-hub/)

Opteran's approach is reverse-engineered insect algorithms (optical flow / navigation / decision), not a connectome-derived SNN. — [The Engineer, 2020](https://www.theengineer.co.uk/articles/opteran-secures-21m-to-solve-autonomy-in-robotics), [Tech.eu, 2022](https://tech.eu/2022/06/24/opteran-weaves-in-12-million-to-develop-ai-driven-by-brain-biomimicry-inspired-by-insects), [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/opteran/)

### Inferences
- Investor-relevant scale: biocomputing/insect-AI startups raised roughly $10-15M each across their lifetimes (Opteran ~$14-15M incl. the 2022 round; Cortical Labs ~$11-12M by aggregator totals; Eon $3.5M seed); a seed-scale ask is consistent with the category.

### Gaps

## 7. What would plausibly be new in a student SO-101 version (my assessment, not a sourced fact)

### Takeaway
Not new: using the FlyWire/MaleCNS connectome as an LIF SNN, or "connectome in a body". Potentially new (as far as I could find as of Nov 2026): a documented, measured, real-hardware closed loop of a fly-connectome SNN driving a manipulator arm (SO-101) via LeRobot, with ablation controls.

### Inferences
- Novelty claims to avoid: "first connectome-controlled robot" (C. elegans LEGO 2014; Fly Brain Bridge, FlyDrones exist), "first embodied fly" (Eon, Mar 2026), "first whole-brain SNN in closed loop" (Eon, hobby repos).
- Defensible narrow claims, if the student actually delivers them: (1) first connectome-driven controller on a low-cost manipulator found in a search of public sources through Nov 2026; (2) measured closed loop with real encoder/servo feedback (the two existing physical projects say their hardware loop is untested/open-loop); (3) controls such as rewired/degree-matched or shuffled-connectome ablations on hardware, which only a few hobby repos do in simulation (e.g., ljmor/flywire-snn-stdp benchmarks against a degree-matched random control: [repo](https://github.com/ljmor/flywire-snn-stdp)); (4) open, cheap, reproducible kit.
- Honest scientific caveat shared with all competitors: sensory encoding and motor decoding are designed, so the task performance cannot be attributed to the connectome without such controls. A preprint (Oct 2026, via secondary coverage) found MaleCNS topology beat rewired controls on two artificial tasks while another study found no consistent advantage on a hashing task — mixed evidence on whether real wiring helps (unverified secondary summary): [howaiworks.ai](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/male-fruit-fly-connectome-google-janelia-2026), [Live Science context](https://livescience.com/health/neuroscience/new-map-of-a-male-fly-central-nervous-system-includes-all-166-000-neurons-and-enables-direct-comparisons-to-female-fly-brain).
