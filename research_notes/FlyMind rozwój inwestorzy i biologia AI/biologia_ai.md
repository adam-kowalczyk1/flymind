## 1. Historic biology -> AI transfers: what exactly came from biology?

### Takeaway
Biology supplied the *starting idea* each time (threshold neuron, hierarchical receptive fields, reward-prediction error, similarity-preserving hashing), but the engineering that made each work (learning rule, backprop, scale) was added by mathematicians/engineers; the honest claim is "inspiration and later partial validation", not "copied from the brain".

### Cited Findings
- McCulloch & Pitts 1943 gave a binary threshold-neuron model with fixed weights and no learning rule; Rosenblatt added a learning rule and weighted inputs (Mark I Perceptron publicly demonstrated 7 July 1958); it could only separate linearly separable patterns (no XOR), and Minsky–Papert's critique is commonly cited for the later slowdown. Source quality: educational pages, date discrepancies exist (some say 1957 for hardware) — REPORTED. — [CSULB history](https://home.csulb.edu/~cwallis/artificialn/History.htm), [Wikipedia: Perceptron](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Perceptron)
- Hubel & Wiesel (1962, cat V1): orientation-selective "simple" cells with local receptive fields and "complex" cells that act like pooling. Fukushima 1980 Neocognitron built S-cells/C-cells on this, with unsupervised layer-wise learning; LeCun et al. 1989 trained such convolutional networks end-to-end with backprop on handwritten digits. Fukushima himself noted the original Hubel–Wiesel hierarchy "does not hold in its original form". REPORTED (search summary of arXiv 1701.05549 and 2001.07092). — [arXiv 1701.05549](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05549), [arXiv 2001.07092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07092), [Fukushima 1980 PDF](https://redwood.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Fukushima1980.pdf)
- Dopamine -> TD learning: Schultz, Dayan, Montague, "A neural substrate of prediction and reward", Science 275:1593–1599 (1997): primate dopamine neurons appear to signal reward-prediction errors, fitting quantitative models of adaptive control (temporal-difference learning). Note direction: TD learning came from RL theory first; the 1997 paper showed biology *matched* it. VERIFIED (abstract via Europe PMC). — [DOI 10.1126/science.275.5306.1593](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)
- Fly olfaction -> hashing: Dasgupta, Stevens, Navlakha, "A neural algorithm for a fundamental computing problem", Science 358(6364):793–796, 1 Nov 2017: fly olfactory circuit implements a variant of locality-sensitive hashing for similarity search; three differences from standard algorithms that could improve similarity search. VERIFIED (abstract). Direction here is biology -> algorithm, but the claimed benefit is on search benchmarks, not a robot. — [DOI 10.1126/science.aam9868](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam9868)
- Insect navigation -> robot model: Stone et al. 2017, Current Biology 27(20):3069–3085, anatomically constrained path-integration/steering circuit in the bee central complex, run on a simulated bee and a robot (details of robot test not read). REPORTED. — [PMC6196076](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6196076), [Sci.News summary](https://www.sci.news/biology/bees-brain-guide-home-05303.html)
- Bee-scale networks: Roper, Fernando, Chittka 2017 PLoS Comput Biol: a model with 8 large-field orientation-sensitive inputs and a single mushroom-body-like layer discriminated complex achromatic patterns; argues cognition needs little neural machinery. REPORTED. — [econpapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pcbi00:1005333)

### Inferences
- Defensible one-liner: "Biology gave AI its first architectures and some of its learning theory; the last decade of progress (scale, backprop, transformers) did not come from new biology." (INFERRED)
- For Explory: cite perceptron/CNN/TD-learning as proof that biology->AI transfers are real, but do not imply they were direct copies.

## 2. Current fly / connectome work: what did each actually show?

### Takeaway
Everything strong is in silico or open-loop; the best-supported claims are that connectivity (+ predicted neurotransmitter sign) predicts some neural responses (Shiu, Lappalainen), and that a realistic fly body can be controlled by RL networks (flybody). No peer-reviewed physical robot driven by a fly connectome was found, and critics explicitly warn that a body that walks proves little about the wiring.

### Cited Findings
- Lappalainen et al., Nature 634(8036):1132–1140, online 11 Sep 2024: 64 optic-lobe cell types with measured connectivity; single-neuron/synapse parameters unknown, tuned by deep learning so the network detects motion; predictions matched neural activity measurements from 26 studies; method works better when connectivity is sparse. Trained rate-style model, not spiking, not embodied. VERIFIED (abstract). — [DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3)
- Shiu et al., Nature 634(8032):210–219, online 2 Oct 2024: whole-adult-fly-brain model from connectome + predicted neurotransmitters; activating taste neurons predicted which downstream neurons respond and are needed to start feeding; activating feeding-region neurons predicted motor neurons that fire, confirmed by optogenetics and behaviour; also predicted taste-type interactions and an antennal-grooming circuit. Authors conclude connectivity + predicted transmitter alone generate testable hypotheses. In silico model, no robot. VERIFIED (abstract). — [DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9)
- Vaxenburg et al. (flybody), Nature 643(8074):1312–1320: whole-body MuJoCo fly model for walking and flight; end-to-end RL controllers give naturalistic locomotion along paths from steering commands; hierarchical control with visual sensors for visually guided flight; open-source. Online 23 Apr 2025, print issue July 2025 (the prior note's "31 Jul 2025" is the print date — correct this). Controllers are NOT connectome-derived. VERIFIED (abstract). — [DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09029-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09029-4)
- NeuroMechFly v2 (Wang-Chen et al., Nature Methods 21(12), online 12 Nov 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02497-y): adds vision, olfaction, ascending feedback, rough terrain, adhesion; FlyGym on MuJoCo. REPORTED (carried from prior note, not re-opened). — [EPFL news](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/simulating-how-fruit-flies-see-smell-and-navigat-7)
- Sandia, arXiv 2508.16792 (v1 22 Aug 2025; Wang, Theilman, Rothganger, Severa, Vineyard, Aimone): full FlyWire connectome (~140k neurons, 50M synapses) fitted onto 12 Intel Loihi 2 chips, statistically validated against reference simulations, speedups "orders of magnitude" vs conventional hardware, larger when activity is sparse. Abstract mentions no robot. VERIFIED (abstract). — [arXiv 2508.16792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16792)
- MICrONS (mouse, not fly): April 2025 package (eight Nature/Nature Methods papers per NIH; ten per Allen Institute) — 1 mm^3 of mouse visual cortex, ~half a billion synapses, >200,000 cells, ~75,000 neurons with calcium imaging; core paper Nature 640:435–447 "Functional connectomics spanning multiple areas of mouse visual cortex"; companion finding: neurons with similar responses are preferentially connected, and recurrent networks trained developed similar connectivity. REPORTED (search summaries). — [Allen Institute](https://alleninstitute.org/news/revealing-the-largest-wiring-diagram-and-functional-map-of-the-brain-through-microns), [NIH](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/understanding-how-visual-information-processed-brain)
- MaleCNS v1.0: Cell, 3 Sep 2026 (Berg et al., doi 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015), ~166,700 neurons, ~125M synapses per Google Research. REPORTED (prior note; not re-opened). — [Live Science](https://livescience.com/health/neuroscience/new-map-of-a-male-fly-central-nervous-system-includes-all-166-000-neurons-and-enables-direct-comparisons-to-female-fly-brain)
- Eon Systems "virtual embodied fly" (10 Mar 2026): Shiu LIF brain + Lappalainen vision + NeuroMechFly v2, 15 ms sync; own stated limits: brain-body mappings chosen by hand, vision "somewhat decorative", internal dynamics not validated against biological signatures, body not driven by full motor hierarchy. REPORTED (prior note quotes Eon page; not re-opened). — [Eon post](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation)
- Critique of "walking = explained": The Transmitter, 2 Apr 2026, reports a Brunton-lab bioRxiv preprint that wired a *C. elegans* connectome into a fly body model and trained it with deep RL to walk ("digital sphinx"); Brunton: RL optimises performance, so it can reproduce fly behaviour with a non-fly brain. Turaga (Janelia): connectome models lack biophysics/neuromodulation, any random network on the body might walk; Cowley: ask whether a randomly wired network gives the same behaviour (a ~300-neuron random network has enough randomness to drive plausible leg movement). Shiu agreed it is not a full copy and suggested "digital twin"/"embodied model". Eon had not published methods then. VERIFIED (article text). — [The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/systems-neuroscience/digital-sphinx-raises-questions-about-connectome-models/)
- Press/commentary: Eon's head of engineering Philip Shiu said "this is not a full blown copy of a fly"; Carboncopies argues task-completion in a simplified environment is not how to validate a brain emulation. REPORTED (search summary). — [Carboncopies](https://carboncopies.org/Blog/Posts/FruitFlyNotUploaded/Post/), [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ybwcxBRrsKavJB9Wz/no-we-haven-t-uploaded-a-fly-yet)
- Robot-hardware status: no Eon robot-controller version or later 2026 update found today; prior note found only hobby "Fly Brain Bridge" (Sesame quadruped, hardware loop not yet demonstrated by author) and no SO-100/101 arm project. REPORTED. — [Fly Brain Bridge](https://www.doriantodd.com/projects/fly-brain-bridge)

(INFERRED; numbers from project brief, not re-verified by me)

## 3. Where could biology give AI something it lacks (energy, few-shot, robustness, reflexes, priors) — evidence quality

### Takeaway
Energy efficiency has the best numbers but they are workload-specific, vendor-driven, and mostly against edge GPUs; few-shot/insect-scale learning is credible as small models (bee, fly hashing) but not shown beating modern AI at robot tasks; fast reflexes and priors are plausible but unproven for connectome controllers.

### Cited Findings
- Intel Hala Point (Apr 2024): up to 2,600 W max, up to 1.15 billion neurons, 128 billion synapses, 140,544 cores, 1,152 Loihi 2 chips; headline 15 TOPS/W measured on one pruned MLP (10:1 sparsity, 10% activation, sigma-delta neurons, tested Apr 2024); generic "100x less energy, 50x faster than CPU/GPU" is Intel's claim with no independent verification found; Intel calls it a research prototype. REPORTED (Intel press release via search). — [Intel Newsroom](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai)
- Loihi 2 vs GPU benchmarks (academic): keyword spotting vs Jetson Orin Nano, ~200x less energy and up to 10x faster, ~250x/18x in a related pipeline; a state-space model at <25 nJ and 0.12 ms per token, 145x energy and 7x latency vs Jetson Orin Nano; sparse coding (LCA) beats conventional devices at high sparsity penalty, compared incl. A100; one lightweight CNN inference comparison 12.5x energy vs NVIDIA T4. Mostly edge GPUs, workload-specific; I only saw search-result summaries. REPORTED. — [arXiv 2307.13762](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13762v1.pdf), [arXiv 2407.20547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.20547), [Emergent Mind overview (aggregator, low reliability)](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/intel-s-loihi-2-neuromorphic-chip)
- Sandia Loihi 2 fly-connectome result (see Q2): speedup grows with sparsity — supports "fly-like sparse recurrent wiring suits neuromorphic hardware" for *simulation speed*, not shown for control quality. VERIFIED (abstract). — [arXiv 2508.16792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16792)
- Few-shot / small-brain learning: fly olfactory hashing (Q1) and the 8-input bee mushroom-body-like model (Q1) show useful similarity search / pattern discrimination with tiny circuits. VERIFIED/REPORTED as in Q1.
- Built-in priors from wiring: Lappalainen shows connectivity constrains models and works better with sparse connectivity (VERIFIED abstract). Whether a wiring prior helps *learning speed* in a control task is not shown by anything I read; the 2026 arXiv items in the prior note (2602.17997 graph controller "sample-efficiency gains", simulation) are REPORTED, not verified by me. — [arXiv 2602.17997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997)
- Competing robot-AI benchmark context (from project brief, REPORTED/unverified by me): SO-101 benchmark arXiv 2606.08881: pi0.5 56%, SmolVLA 32.5%. Existing project report on VLA limits: /Users/admin/so101_fly_project/flymind/reports/robot_ai_revolution_20261112.md and bio_advantage_research_20261110.md.

### Inferences
- Rank by evidence quality for a pitch (INFERRED): (1) wiring-validated mechanisms and honest controls — strong; (2) neuromorphic speed/energy for sparse spiking — real but vendor-heavy and not specific to FlyMind (FlyMind runs on a MacBook, ~1.2% of neurons; median 1.29 ms touch->command per project brief); (3) few-shot/robustness/priors — hypotheses only. Do NOT claim energy savings for FlyMind: nothing was measured on neuromorphic hardware.
- Energy claim hygiene: always quote hardware, workload, baseline GPU, and whether the number is vendor-reported.

## 4. Why FlyMind makes sense despite classical control winning; limits of the argument; honest biology message for Explory

### Takeaway
The honest case is scientific and educational, not commercial: FlyMind is a small closed-loop test bench that asks "does the wiring from data matter?" with shuffle/mirror controls, which is precisely the control critics of connectome-to-body demos say is missing; it should be presented as sim-only, ~1.2% of the neurons, with classical control still better or equal on the tasks tested.

### Cited Findings
- Field context: the Transmitter article (2 Apr 2026) quotes Turaga, Cowley and Brunton calling for random/shuffled-network controls on connectome-driven bodies. VERIFIED. — [The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/systems-neuroscience/digital-sphinx-raises-questions-about-connectome-models/)
- Eon admits hand-chosen brain-body mappings and "decorative" vision (REPORTED, Q2) — an interface-design weakness FlyMind shares and must declare. — [Eon post](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation)
- Project facts (from the task brief; verify in repo before posting): vision loop LC10a->DNa02 base rotation data net 38/40, shuffled 3/40, mirrored 0/40, P-controller 40/40; grasp with T2L leg circuit: classical 93/net 90 (soft contact), classical 101/net 84 (stiff), 10 strength-matched shuffles 101,82,80,102,83,79,82,79,99,4 vs 90 for data; vision+touch classical 98 vs net 86; 12 Nov 2026 Adam ruled colour encoder->LC10a is artificial stimulation. Files: /Users/admin/so101_fly_project/research_notes/Projekt mózgu muchy dla inwestorów/nasze_wyniki.md and flymind/reports/.

### Inferences
- Why it makes sense (INFERRED): (a) SCIENCE — closed-loop, falsifiable tests of connectome hypotheses with strength-matched shuffles; negative results (grasp: wiring gives no advantage) are publishable-quality honesty; (b) ENGINEERING — an auditable pipeline from open data (MaleCNS CC-BY) to a physical-arm interface; useful as a test bench for sparse-spiking/neuromorphic ideas later; (c) EDUCATION — a student learns neuroscience, control, statistics and experimental honesty on a real open dataset.
- Limits (INFERRED, must be stated): all results MuJoCo; no real-arm test; classical P-controller is more precise (40/40); wiring not shown to help grasp; sensory input partly artificial (colour encoder), optic lobe replaced; learning circuits unused; ~1.2% of neurons; single-lab, student-scale; no energy or robustness claim; the 2026 literature shows others do similar sim coupling, so novelty lies in the controls and the arm-facing interface, not in running a connectome.
- Honest message (PROPOSED, Polish for the poster): "Czy okablowanie mózgu muchy ma znaczenie, gdy steruje ramieniem? Sprawdziliśmy to w symulacji z kontrolami (wymieszane i odwrócone połączenia). W pętli wzrokowej prawdziwe połączenia działają (38/40 vs 3/40 po wymieszaniu), ale zwykły regulator jest dokładniejszy, a przy chwytaniu przewagi okablowania nie widać. To uczciwy test, nie dowód, że mucha jest lepsza od AI. Dotąd tylko symulacja, jedno ramię czeka na test." Do NOT write: "mucha steruje robotem", "mucha lepsza od AI", "uploaded fly", energy savings.
- Biology-revolutionises-AI framing (PROPOSED, bounded): say biology has repeatedly *seeded* AI (perceptron, CNN, TD, fly hashing) and that connectomes now let us *test* which circuit features matter; the revolution is an open hypothesis, not a result.
