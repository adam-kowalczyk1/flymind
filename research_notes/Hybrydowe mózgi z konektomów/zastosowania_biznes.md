## 2. Evidence for collision avoidance, event-based low latency, neuromorphic flight and insect-inspired navigation in industry

### Takeaway
Academic evidence for LGMD-style collision avoidance on small robots and for fully neuromorphic drone control on Loihi is real (TU Delft: 200 Hz closed loop, ~7-12 mW incremental network power), but I found no commercial LGMD product or industrial deployment; event cameras do have industrial product traction (IDS/Prophesee), while insect-navigation deployments (warehouse/mining) remain pilots at best.

### Cited Findings
- LGMD reviews (2019, 2025): LGMD-based models have improved collision-free navigation in ground and aerial robots (Khepera, Colias, hexapod, small quadcopter); a 2025 paper reports embedded vision implementations on micro robots/quadcopters (an aggregator summary claims <15 ms warning latency and >40% false-positive reduction - NOT verified against the paper). Sources flag open debates about LGMD processing; "no evidence of commercial products or industrial deployment" found in my search — [arXiv 2501.02982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02982v2); [arXiv 1904.02048 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02048); [Frontiers Neurorobotics 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2024.1349498/full).
- TU Delft (Science Robotics, May 2024): fully neuromorphic vision-to-control pipeline on a drone; control layer is one decoding layer trained by evolution in simulation; runs on Loihi at 200 Hz with 0.94 W idle power plus ~7-12 mW for the network; embedded GPU ran the same network ~25 Hz (university says ~10-64x difference). Tasks: hover, landing, lateral flight with yaw. Earlier: 93-neuron network for altitude control on a quadrotor with Loihi. I read abstract/press only — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:6cbe83a3-1f86-48b0-b478-88e71f807546); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1044784); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/neuromorphic-ai-autonomous-drone-26114).
- Event cameras in industry: IDS industrial camera series with Prophesee sensor (2025); Fortune Business Insights says industrial automation & robotics is the largest segment (27.3% share in 2026) of the event-camera market — [Meticulous Research](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/meticulous-next/event-based-vision-outlook-2026-2036); [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/segmentation/event-camera-market-110552).
- Opteran positions its optical-flow collision signals as a backup layer behind a human operator (ODK-2) rather than a primary safety function — [eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/dev-board-for-insect-like-visual-ai).
- Opteran pilots reportedly with customers in US/South Africa/Europe (cobots, drones, mining robots) per aggregator notes in earlier file (secondary, 2022-era) - [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/opteran/) (carried over from rynek_inwestorzy.md; not re-verified).

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence class available is "closed loop at X Hz, Y mW on neuromorphic silicon vs embedded GPU". For our fly-connectome controller the equivalent would need: a hardware port (Loihi/Speck/Akida/FPGA) and measured power - not available from simulation on a laptop. HIPOTEZA: an LC-neuron-based looming reflex is a natural niche, but the same function is cheaply done by optical-flow/time-to-contact classical vision or a ToF/radar sensor; the bio-inspired option must beat these on cost/latency/power, which no source I found demonstrates.
- Event-camera success shows the sensor side of "bio-inspired low latency" can sell without SNNs; the SNN controller layer remains unproven commercially.

### Gaps
- No independent field-trial data (warehouse, mining) for insect-inspired navigation; no comparative benchmarks vs classical optical flow/TTC for collision avoidance in industry; Delft paper full text not read.

## 3. Application-domain analysis (problem, incumbents, limits, SNN/bio advantage, evidence needed, deployment difficulty)

### Takeaway
Across all domains the only plausible near-term niches are (a) ultra-low-power, low-latency reflex layers (collision/looming, withdrawal) on small drones/edge sensors, and (b) research/education platforms; for manipulation, industrial robots and mainstream mobile robots, classical control, MPC and learned policies/VLAs already work and the bio-inspired value proposition is unevidenced. Everything marked HIPOTEZA below is my reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- Industrial robot base is huge and conventional: IFR reports a record ~5 mln operational industrial robots in 2025 (+9%); 2024: 4,663,698 stock and 542,076 installations; 2025 installation numbers conflict between secondary reports (~354k vs "more than 600k") - check IFR directly — [IFR World Robotics 2025 press](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years); [Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/article/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/5-million-robots-now-working-factories-worldwide-ifr-reports/).
- Robotics VC in 2026 flows to learned/foundation approaches: e.g., Standard Bots US$200 mln at US$1 bn valuation for "AI-native" industrial arms (Jun 2026); Apptronik >US$935 mln Series A incl. 520 mln extension (Feb 2026); Mytra US$120 mln Series C (Jan 2026); Trener Robotics US$32 mln Series A (adaptive industrial robots) — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/09/standard-bots-raises-200m-1b-valuation-revolutionize-ai-native-industrial-robotics/); [Mammoth Times (Apptronik)](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/gnwcq-2026-2-11-apptronik-closes-over-935-million-series-a-with-new-520-million-extension-round); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/15/mytra-raises-120-million-series-c-scale-supply-chain-robotics/); [Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/norways-trener-robotics-closes-32-million-series-a-to-turn-industrial-robots-into-adaptive-teammates-dc6cecf7-e5d9-4a8e-a9bf-cc2d3fa50ae6) (secondary news; investor money is in learned/foundation approaches, not SNN).
- Safety certification: an Infosys blog (vendor/consultancy opinion) states no neuromorphic processor had met ISO 26262/ASIL requirements as of 2025 and estimates 3-7 years for safety-critical roles; a preprint argues SNN compilers lack WCET evidence/documentation for IEC 62304 Class B; Drexel work notes sparse event-driven computation can hide synaptic faults; a TU Delft 2025 paper reviews trustworthiness of SNNs; an arXiv paper argues current AI governance is a poor fit for SNNs with local learning (hard to audit/benchmark) — [Infosys blog](https://blogs.infosys.com/emerging-technology-solutions/artificial-intelligence/brains-on-wheels-the-role-of-neuromorphic-computing-in-next-gen-mobility.html); [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10249388); [Drexel](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/991022068695404721); [TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:fb12b1c9-c439-42a8-87f1-478c1630212e); [arXiv 2602.01503](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01503).
- Energy/latency evidence on the sensor-to-control path exists only for narrow tasks (Delft drone, Section 2) and vendor claims (Innatera "500x less energy, 100x faster" - own claim, from rynek_inwestorzy.md).

### Inferences (per domain; all HIPOTEZA unless tied to a finding above)
| Domain | Real problem | Incumbents | Limits of incumbents | Possible SNN/bio advantage (HIPOTEZA) | Evidence needed | Deployment difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manipulation (arms, SO-101 class) | Dexterous, adaptive grasping in unstructured settings | Classical IK/PID, MPC, imitation/deep RL, VLAs (pi-style) | Data hunger, compute, poor interpretability/latency of big VLAs | Small interpretable reflex/low-level layer under a VLA (e.g., contact reflexes) | Head-to-head vs PID/MPC and a small learned policy on same tasks, success rate, latency, power | Low-level safety-critical joint control is conservative; learned layers need certification-grade guarantees |

| Drones (micro/nano) | Tiny power/compute budget; fast reflexes | Onboard classical flight controllers, small CNNs on MCU/GPU | Power/weight limits on <100 g platforms | Event-driven low-power control (Delft: 200 Hz, mW-level on Loihi) | Repeat on commercially available silicon with outdoor flights; battery-life gain in minutes | Loihi not commercially sold; Speck/Akida/Pulsar toolchains immature |
| Industrial robots | Cycle time, precision, safety | PLC/servo PID, MPC, certified safety controllers | Little pain; behaviour is deterministic and certified | Virtually none identified | N/A - low priority | Certification (ISO 10218 etc.) and customer conservatism - classical already sufficient |

| Small edge-AI devices | Always-on sensing at µW-mW | MCU with TinyML, DSP | Energy per inference, latency | Spiking MCUs (Pulsar, Speck, Akida) - best-evidenced commercial route, mostly for audio/vision classification not robot control | Third-party benchmarks vs Cortex-M+NPU on real workloads | Tooling lock-in, small talent pool |
| Energy-constrained systems (space, wearables, implantables) | Battery/thermal budget | Low-power MCUs | Limited | Event-driven sparsity may reduce energy | Measured system-level power, not chip-level peak claims | Qualification costs |
| Low-latency systems | Sub-ms to ms loops | FPGA/ASIC, high-rate classical control, event cameras | Hard real-time already solvable classically | Event sensors+SNN might shorten perception latency | Latency under load vs FPGA baseline | Few engineers know both worlds |

## 5. Business models and the research-tools market

### Takeaway
Revenue from neuroscience/robotics-research software is typically small: free open-source simulators dominate, money comes from grants, hardware kits and services. The least-bad models are (1) grant-funded open platform + paid support/education kits and (2) selling a reproducible benchmark/controller library to robotics R&D; software licensing for a controller is unproven (Opteran pricing undisclosed; BrainChip licensing = US$0.6 mln in a half-year).

### Cited Findings
- Hardware-anchored research/education revenue exists but is small: Backyard Brains ~US$500k revenue after five years, 10,000 SpikerBoxes by 2016, NIMH SBIR ~US$450k; Cortical Labs CL1 priced US$35,000 (rack 30 units at US$20,000 each) — [Backyard Brains blog](https://blog.backyardbrains.com/2017/05/the-3000000-spike-byb-sales-milestone/); [NIMH SBIR](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/funding/sbir/backyard-brains-innovative-products-to-help-kids-learn-about-neuroscience); [DatacenterDynamics](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/australian-startup-cortical-labs-unveils-worlds-first-commercial-biological-computer) (carried over from rynek_inwestorzy.md; older, not re-verified).
- BrainChip's mix (licences + dev services + products) shows the typical neuromorphic revenue model: IP licences and engineering services dominate over product sales (H1 2026 numbers in Section 1).
- Opteran's model: software evaluated "on your platform" with undisclosed pricing (likely licence/engineering per integration; not stated) — [opteran.com](https://opteran.com).
- Eon Systems: seed-funded brain-emulation science company, no product revenue reported in sources — [Untapped Ventures](https://untappedventures.substack.com/p/untapped-ventures-invests-in-eon).
- Intel Lava is open-source (neuromorphic framework) — free tools are the norm, which reduces willingness to pay for software — [remio / general coverage](https://www.remio.ai/post/neuromorphic-computing-chipset-adoption-intel-hala-point-and-emerging-industry-trends) (weak source).

### Inferences (HIPOTEZA unless noted)
- Software licensing: weak - open datasets (FlyWire connectome is public, CC-BY per the project's brief) give no data moat; competitors (Eon, academic labs, NeuroMechFly) release free code. Value would have to lie in integration, validation and support.
- Research tooling SaaS (NeuroMechFly-style): labs mostly use free tools (the task brief states NeuroMechFly and Brian are free; not independently re-verified here); revenue would be grants/consulting/hosted compute, probably tens of thousands of USD per customer at best.
- Controller library: sellable to robotics R&D labs only if benchmarked against PID/MPC/learned baselines with energy/latency numbers - currently missing.
- Education kits: realistic, small (Backyard Brains scale: ~US$100k-1 mln/yr), and fits a student project; a "fly-brain-controls-robot-arm" kit based on SO-101 (~US$130) is an HIPOTEZA niche.
- Vertical solutions (e.g., inspection robots in hazardous plants): large markets (ANYbotics scale) but a connectome controller is not a differentiator customers ask for; high capital and certification burden.

### Gaps
- Not researched in this pass: actual license prices/revenues of commercial neuroscience software (MBF Bioscience, Neuralynx, NEURON/Brian ecosystem), typical grant sizes (NSF/NIH/ERC/Horizon Europe neuromorphic calls), NeuroMechFly funding. ## 6. Customers, what investors require, barriers to entry, and kill risks

### Takeaway
Credible early customers are neuroscience/robotics research labs and education; defense is excluded by the project's rule (competition ethics); drone makers and edge-chip vendors are potential partners but only after silicon-level evidence. Investors will want measured advantage vs classical baselines on real hardware, a named pilot customer and a defensible moat - the project has none of the three yet. The most likely killers: classical controllers/learned policies already sufficient, no accessible neuromorphic hardware, no IP moat, better-funded Eon/academia.

### Cited Findings
- Learned/foundation approaches attract the large rounds (Section 3) while neuromorphic firms report small revenue (BrainChip) - the capital market signals which approach is winning commercially — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/09/standard-bots-raises-200m-1b-valuation-revolutionize-ai-native-industrial-robotics/); [Motley Fool AU](https://www.fool.com.au/2026/08/26/brainchip-shares-half-year-results-show-revenue-up-loss-widens/).
- Neuromorphic hardware availability: Hala Point not commercially available (research prototype); BrainChip's next chip AKD2500 only at late-2026 milestone; Innatera/SynSense customers not verifiable — Sections 1.
- Competition: Eon Systems already demonstrates a fly-connectome-driven embodied emulation (Mar 2026) with seed funding; Delft shows fully neuromorphic drone control — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/16/digital_fruit_fly_brain_model/); [TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:6cbe83a3-1f86-48b0-b478-88e71f807546).
- Certification/tooling/trust barriers for SNNs — Section 3 sources (Infosys, preprint, Drexel, TU Delft, arXiv).
- Talent: Innatera and VLSI Expert announced a programme to build a neuromorphic talent pipeline (Jan 2026) - indicates scarcity of trained engineers (low-quality source; the announcement itself not verified) — [Mammoth Times](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/tokenring-2026-1-23-the-neuromorphic-revolution-innatera-and-vlsi-expert-launch-global-talent-pipeline-for-brain-inspired-chips).

### Inferences (HIPOTEZA)
- Potential customers by order of realism: (1) academic neuroscience/robotics labs and teaching (needs reproducible code + benchmark); (2) neuromorphic chip vendors as ecosystem demo/reference-app partners (they need compelling robotics demos - BrainChip/Innatera marketing; unconfirmed demand); (3) micro-drone/edge-sensor makers after hardware-level evidence; (4) defense - explicitly out of scope per the competition ethics rule; note defence is a common first buyer of neuromorphic autonomy, so excluding it narrows the market further.
- Investor evidence ladder: (a) baseline-matched benchmark (same task, PID/MPC/small-RL/VLA) showing at least parity with a stated advantage; (b) energy/latency measured on actual SNN silicon; (c) robustness/fault tolerance (noise, sensor dropout, damage) where a connectome structure might help; (d) one external pilot user; (e) IP/data position.
- IP/moat: connectome data are public (FlyWire CC-BY) so no data moat; protectable assets are code, validated parameter sets/tuning, benchmark suites, brand, community - weak.
