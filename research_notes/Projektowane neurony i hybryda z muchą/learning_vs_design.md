# Topic 1b: Learning vs designing spiking controllers for robots, and how honestly results are measured (2026-11-13)

Evidence labels: VERIFIED = existence/metadata of the publication checked by us (arXiv/Crossref/Europe PMC resolved); REPORTED = result stated by authors, with what we actually read in brackets; INFERRED = our reasoning; NOT VERIFIED = could not open/confirm. All content paraphrased. Nothing in the FlyMind repo was modified; no simulation run.

## 1. How are spiking-controller weights obtained (hand design, NEF, STDP/R-STDP, surrogate gradient/BPTT, e-prop, evolution, ANN-to-SNN, deep RL actors, imitation), and what are the pros/cons for a 6-DOF arm?

### Takeaway
Almost every SNN controller that shows competitive arm/locomotion numbers got its weights from gradient-based training (deep RL with a spiking actor, or surrogate-gradient BPTT), in simulation, with the SNN compared to a non-spiking network of the same shape, not to PID/IK/MPC. Hand-designed or biologically wired controllers (NEF, LGMD, Braitenberg-style, our connectome) are typically shown on low-dimensional tasks, and their parameters are usually tuned by hand or by optimiser, which is a form of training that papers rarely count as such.

### Cited Findings
- Survey of SNN robot control classified by learning rule (STDP, R-STDP, RL, evolutionary, supervised/conversion), noting the lack of practical training methods as the main limit (Bing et al. 2018, Front. Neurorobotics; VERIFIED existence, REPORTED content, read: abstract via Europe PMC) — [DOI 10.3389/fnbot.2018.00035](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbot.2018.00035)
- Deep RL with a population-coded spiking actor (PopSAN) plus an ANN critic: trained with DDPG/TD3/SAC/PPO on MuJoCo gym tasks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, Ant), network = input/output populations of 10 neurons per dimension and two 256-neuron LIF layers; 10 random seeds per algorithm, 1M training steps, each evaluation 10 episodes up to 1000 steps. Max average reward on Loihi vs full-precision GPU actor: HalfCheetah 10505 vs 10729, Hopper 3289 vs 3599, Walker2d 4280 vs 4995, Ant 5220 vs 5416 (Tang et al. 2020, CoRL; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED numbers, read: ar5iv full-text summary via fetch tool) — [arXiv 2010.09635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09635)
- PopSAN's baselines include the same-architecture deep actor and DNN-to-SNN conversion (hard/soft reset, T=5/15/25) and a single-neuron-rate-coding SNN (RateSAN); it needed layer-wise weight rescaling on Loihi (REPORTED, read: ar5iv summary) — [arXiv 2010.09635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09635)
- Follow-up PopSAN on Franka arm (Isaac Gym, PixMC pick-and-place-type tasks): claims up to 96.10% energy saving vs ANN at comparable control performance; simulation only (2025; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [arXiv 2510.10516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10516)
- Fully spiking LIF controller trained end-to-end with surrogate gradients + forward model (predictive control) on a planar 2D arm and a simulated 6-DOF Franka Panda with torque control; 10 seeds, 64 fixed random initial/target states for the 3D task; baselines were non-spiking recurrent nets (0.8M and 12.7M parameters) and a plain SNN; SNN (~0.9M params) reported as matching the large baseline on distance/success/time-on-target with ~14x fewer parameters; no comparison with classical controllers; BPTT is memory-heavy and hyperparameter-sensitive; simulation only; no hardware (Huebotter et al. 2025; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: HTML text, first 100k of ~228k characters, exact 3D numbers only in a figure we did not see) — [arXiv 2509.05356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05356)
- Surrogate-gradient learning is the standard recipe for training spiking networks with backprop-type methods despite non-differentiable spikes (Neftci, Mostafa, Zenke 2019; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, title/metadata only) — [arXiv 1901.09948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09948)
- e-prop: a biologically plausible online approximation of BPTT for recurrent spiking nets, reported to approach BPTT performance and suggested for on-chip learning (Bellec et al. 2020, Nat. Commun.; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract via Europe PMC) — [DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-17236-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17236-y)
- ANN-to-SNN conversion: conversion of VGG-16/Inception-v3 etc. to spiking nets; reports that SNNs trade error rate against operations (about 2x fewer operations for a few points higher error on LeNet/BinaryNet). Image classification, not control (Rueckauer et al. 2017; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [DOI 10.3389/fnins.2017.00682](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2017.00682)
- R-STDP vs "indirect" (DQN policy transferred to SNN by supervised learning) for a lane-keeping vehicle with an event camera, three scenarios; R-STDP reported better on lateral localisation accuracy and training steps vs three other algorithms of the paper; a small vehicle, not an arm (Bing et al. 2020, Neural Networks; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [DOI 10.1016/j.neunet.2019.05.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2019.05.019)
- Nengo/NEF as "white-box" compilation of known functions into spiking populations; demonstrations: a rover steered by a CNN + spiking control in MuJoCo, and neural adaptive control added to a force-based operational-space controller on a real Kinova Jaco2 arm reaching task; the arm demo augments a classical controller rather than replacing it (DeWolf et al. 2020; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [arXiv 2007.10227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10227)
- Biologically inspired spiking joint controller (short-term plasticity, gain-regulating neurons), deployable on Loihi, scaled to real robot arms; reported 19% lower jerk, "state-of-the-art" control performance (Polykretis, Supic, Danielescu 2022; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract only) — [arXiv 2209.02787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02787)
- SNN adaptive arm controller whose parameters came from an extensive search; arm learns tasks 15% faster in selected scenarios (six or nine random targets) (Supic, Stewart 2022; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [arXiv 2210.01243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01243)
- Evolutionary / Bayesian parameter search used for an LGMD spiking model because analog neuromorphic hardware is noisy: Differential Evolution, Self-Adaptive DE, Bayesian Optimisation, validated on DVS recordings from a UAV (Salt, Howard, Indiveri, Sandamirskaya 2019; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract via fetch) — [arXiv 1910.07960](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07960)
- Other sim-only arm examples: fully spiking actor-critic with PPO for a 9-DOF arm reaching/grasping in Isaac Gym with a *theoretical* energy model (2025; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [arXiv 2508.12038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12038); cerebellum-inspired vision-based control (2020; REPORTED, abstract not read in detail) — [arXiv 2011.01641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01641)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: For a 6-DOF arm, the only SNN approaches with published multi-joint numbers (Huebotter, PopSAN-Franka) rely on gradient training with simulated data in the 1e5-1e6 step range; they are not "no training". Our connectome network, with fixed data wiring and no weight learning, is in the "hand design/biological wiring" family; its closest published relatives (NEF/Nengo, LGMD models) use either known-function compilation or tuned parameters.
- INFERRED: Surrogate-gradient BPTT or e-prop could in principle be applied to a connectome-constrained network (training only gains or a readout while keeping the topology), but the rule 3 Nov ("the fly must steer") would have to say whether trained gains count as the fly deciding. I found no source testing this on MaleCNS; see other topic notes.
- INFERRED: Nobody in this set reports sim-to-real transfer of a trained multi-joint SNN arm policy; the real-robot examples (Jaco2 adaptive control, Polykretis arm) are low-level controllers augmenting or replacing joint loops.

### Gaps
- Full text of Polykretis 2022 and Supic 2022 not read, so real-arm trial counts and baselines are unknown.
- Did not find a peer-reviewed R-STDP arm-reaching study with trial counts (searches returned only locomotion/lane-keeping); not confirmed that none exists.
- No source found that applies gradient training to a connectome-constrained network for robot control; not confirmed absent.

## 2. Honest head-to-head comparisons of SNN controllers vs classical controllers (PID/IK/MPC) or ANNs: who reports trial counts, baselines, ablations; typical gaps

### Takeaway
Comparisons are overwhelmingly SNN vs ANN with the same architecture on RL benchmarks (good: seeds, ablations; bad: no classical controller, no real arm). Head-to-head against PID/IK/MPC is rare in what I found; where a classical controller is a component (Nengo Jaco2) it is augmented, not beaten. The RL-evaluation literature gives the standard for what "honest" should include.

### Cited Findings
- PopSAN paper reports 10 seeds x 4 algorithms and several SNN baselines (conversion, rate coding), but energy was measured on a single task (HalfCheetah) and few platforms (REPORTED, read: ar5iv summary) — [arXiv 2010.09635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09635)
- Huebotter et al.: ablation of initialisation, learnable time constants, adaptive thresholds, latent compression; baseline = non-spiking recurrent nets; no classical controller reported in the text we read (REPORTED, partial read) — [arXiv 2509.05356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05356)
- Deep RL reproducibility: results are hard to interpret because of environment randomness and algorithm variance; calls for significance measures and standardised reporting and baselines (Henderson et al., AAAI 2018; VERIFIED existence via arXiv page and Crossref; REPORTED, read: abstract-level summary) — [arXiv 1709.06560](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560), [DOI 10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11694](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11694)
- Few-run comparisons with point estimates can mislead; authors recommend interval estimates, performance profiles, interquartile mean (Agarwal et al. 2021; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract-level summary) — [arXiv 2108.13264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)
- NeuroBench: community framework (about 100 contributors) with algorithm track (hardware-independent) and system track, motivated by the absence of accepted benchmarks that makes it hard to compare neuromorphic and conventional methods (Yik et al. 2023; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract via fetch) — [arXiv 2304.04640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04640)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Typical gap pattern: (a) SNN vs same-shape ANN gap is small (PopSAN: about 2-14% lower max reward depending on task, Loihi vs GPU, from table figures above; arithmetic ours); (b) rarely any PID/IK baseline; (c) simulation only; (d) tuned on the evaluation task. Our own trio "P-controller 40/40, network 38/40, trivial relay 20/20" is more honest than most of this literature in including a trivial baseline, which is exactly what is usually missing.
- INFERRED: Henderson/Agarwal standards translate directly: report per-seed distributions, confidence intervals, number of independent evaluation seeds distinct from tuning seeds. Our 40-trial binomial results have wide intervals (e.g. 38/40 vs 40/40 not separable); I did not compute CIs for the repo (no data run).

### Gaps
- No quantitative head-to-head SNN vs PID/MPC on a manipulator found in the sources opened; this may be a search limitation (searches limited to arXiv/Crossref/Europe PMC and web snippets).
- Aitsam et al. 2022 review (IEEE Access) exists but its text could not be read (IEEE page returned HTTP 418; PDF text extraction failed), so its criticisms are not recorded here.

## 3. Energy/latency claims on neuromorphic hardware (Loihi, SpiNNaker, DYNAP-SE, Akida) for control and their caveats

### Takeaway
Big energy ratios (about 100x) exist for small spiking actors on Loihi vs embedded GPU/CPU, but they come from one task, dynamic power only, partial host accounting, and a favourable ANN baseline; analytical studies show rate-coded SNNs often fail to beat equal-capacity quantised ANNs once memory/data movement is counted. I found nothing quantitative on SpiNNaker, DYNAP-SE, or Akida control tasks in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- PopSAN energy per inference: Loihi about 11.98 microjoule vs Jetson TX2 efficient mode about 1671 microjoule (about 140x), TX2 performance mode about 2347, CPU about 7910, GPU about 12174 microjoule; speed: Loihi about 226 inferences/s vs TX2 about 438 (efficient) and 750 (performance), CPU about 7450, GPU about 3782/s. Energy = dynamic power / inference rate, idle power not used; Loihi power averaged over 8 networks running at once on a Nahuku board; the host computes the first encoding phase and the final action, and it is not clear whether host work is in the Loihi figure (REPORTED, read: ar5iv summary) — [arXiv 2010.09635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09635)
- So the 140x figure is not a latency win: in this paper Loihi was slower per inference than the TX2 and the CPU/GPU (same table; INFERRED from figures above).
- Digital-hardware analysis: with identical silicon technology, most rate-coded SNN implementations will not be more energy- or resource-efficient than the original ANN; "more imaginative uses of spikes" are needed (Davidson & Furber 2021; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract via Europe PMC) — [DOI 10.3389/fnins.2021.651141](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2021.651141)
- Analytical re-assessment: many SNN energy comparisons skip memory access and on-chip network traffic; against matched-capacity quantised networks an SNN needs an average spike rate below roughly 5.7% at time window T=5 (Yan, Bai, Tang, Wong; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract-level summary via fetch) — [arXiv 2409.08290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08290)
- Same group of concern, earlier paper: "Are SNNs Really More Energy-Efficient Than ANNs? An In-Depth Hardware-Aware Study" (Dampfhoffer, Mesquida, Valentian, Anghel; IEEE TETCI 7(3), 2023): existence confirmed via dblp/OpenAlex snippets, exact figures NOT VERIFIED (original text not read) — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FTETCI.2022.3214509)
- Loihi survey: abstract says feedforward deep nets show modest or no benefit on Loihi, while brain-inspired recurrent/sparse/precise-timing networks can show orders-of-magnitude lower latency and energy on some computations (Davies et al. 2021, Proc. IEEE; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract via search summary; full text not read) — [DOI 10.1109/JPROC.2021.3067593](https://doi.org/10.1109/JPROC.2021.3067593)
- Energy benchmarking is niche: one 2025 survey reportedly found only 9 of 111 papers presenting energy metrics for neuromorphic AI (REPORTED via search-engine summary of the paper, NOT VERIFIED against its full text; the paper at [arXiv 2506.09599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09599) is about implantables and I did not open it)
- 2024 perspective on embodied neuromorphic AI for robotics lists fair/representative benchmarks among its challenges (Putra et al.; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract) — [arXiv 2404.03325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03325)
- Real-world SNN implementations differ: analog neuromorphic hardware has noise/variability that forces parameter optimisation (Salt et al. 2019) — [arXiv 1910.07960](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07960)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Energy claims are not relevant to FlyMind's goal (an SO-101 arm on a laptop-class host, sim-only). Any pitch line like "energy-efficient" would be unsupported: our network runs as a dense simulation on a CPU.
- INFERRED: With a rate-coded or dense-simulated ALIF network of 1931 neurons the argument of Davidson/Furber applies against any efficiency claim without neuromorphic hardware.

### Gaps
- No primary data found on SpiNNaker, DYNAP-SE, Akida for arm control; Loihi caveats beyond PopSAN not read (Davies full text not read).
- Host-overhead accounting standards: not found in any source opened.

## 4. What do claims like "X% of collisions avoided without training" usually mean (LGMD/looming, Braitenberg), typical setups, numbers, and what is missing?

### Takeaway
In the LGMD literature such claims mean a hand-built, biologically inspired detector (no learned weights) triggering an avoidance reflex; the number comes from a few arena tests on a small robot, the parameters are set by hand or by optimiser on similar scenarios, and baselines are rare. "No training" nearly always means "no data-driven training", not "no tuning". The video's 98% caption remains NOT VERIFIED.

### Cited Findings
- Review of LGMD-based robotics: the only explicit success rate in the part we read is 95.3% for an LGMD2 model on the Colias robot in arena tests (credited to Fu et al. 2016); most other results are qualitative demos (trajectory plots, one hexapod covering 235.3 m in a 5x8 m room, Colias in a 3x4 m arena with 18 obstacles at 17.9 cm/s); simulation/MATLAB results are common in early work; evolutionary/GA tuning of LGMD models is cited (Yue et al.; Fu 2020; Salt et al.); the review reports no standardised benchmark and only qualitative comparison with non-bio methods; it states that models struggle in diverse, noisy real scenes (Qin, Peng, Yue 2025; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: HTML first 100k of 128k characters via fetch tool) — [arXiv 2501.02982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02982)
- A 2024 Biomimetics paper is reported (by a search-engine summary) to reach "97.51%" collision-avoidance success on a micro robot with an optimised LGMD model: NOT VERIFIED (the DOAJ page returned HTTP 403, I could not read the paper, trial count and baselines unknown) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/4f70f8576bea45a78e60e65dd6cc6de7)
- LGMD models struggle to separate looming from complex background motion during agile flight; a model with distributed excitation/delayed inhibition improves selectivity (Zhao et al. 2020; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract; "systematic experiments" with no numbers in abstract) — [arXiv 2005.04397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04397)
- UAV LGMD competitive strategy (Zhao et al. 2019; VERIFIED existence; abstract not read) — [arXiv 1904.07206](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07206)
- Spiking LGMD on DVS data from a UAV: the search summary says models did well on simulated looming but less well on real stimuli (from a search-engine paraphrase of Salt et al.; NOT VERIFIED beyond that; abstract I read says parameters need optimisation because of noisy hardware) — [arXiv 1910.07960](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07960)
- Looming-detector extension from LGMD to LPLC2 (the fly's looming neuron type; relevant to our eye plan): OppLoD (Shuang et al. 2023; VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, abstract title level) — [arXiv 2302.10284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10284)
- Braitenberg-style spiking reflex studies: I searched and found none with verified trial counts in the sources opened (NOT VERIFIED).

### Inferences
- INFERRED: A caption "98% of collisions avoided without training" is consistent with this literature's pattern: a fixed hand-built reflex circuit, a modest arena test, and a success definition chosen by the authors. Missing items to ask for: number of trials and obstacle configurations, whether obstacle layouts differ from those used when setting parameters, success criterion, a trivial baseline (e.g. stop when proximity sensor trips, or random walk), speed range, and sim vs real.
- INFERRED: Hidden training: when thresholds, gains, time constants and delays are tuned by hand/GA until the demo works, the tuning set is the test set; "without training" then means only "without weight learning". Our own project has the same risk in the vision loop (tuned encoder/gain, 40 trials), which the red-team shuffle/relay controls exposed.
- INFERRED: Looming reflexes are lateralised by construction (obstacle on the left leads to a turn to the right); a left->left relay or a mirror swap solves a lot of the task, which matches our red-team finding that results measure lateralisation. No source here controls for it; the LGMD literature controls neither for topology nor for simple relay baselines in what I read.

### Gaps
- The video's 98% claim: no trial count, controls, or paper found (we have not watched or transcribed the video; the caption is only given by the project owner). NOT VERIFIED.
- Real LGMD success numbers other than 95.3% (secondary, via review) and 97.51% (NOT VERIFIED) not obtained; original Fu et al. 2016 not opened.

## 5. Recent reviews (2022-2026) critiquing evaluation practice in neuromorphic robotics: main criticisms (paraphrased)

### Takeaway
The criticisms I could verify are: no accepted benchmarks (NeuroBench, Putra et al.), neglect of memory/data movement in energy claims (Yan et al., Davidson & Furber), incomparable ad-hoc tests in LGMD robotics (Qin et al.), and RL-style statistical weakness (few seeds, point estimates). A dedicated critical review of neuromorphic *arm* manipulation was not found.

### Cited Findings
- No widely accepted benchmarks exist, which makes it hard to measure progress or compare with conventional approaches; earlier efforts lacked uptake (NeuroBench; 2023) — [arXiv 2304.04640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04640)
- Embodied neuromorphic robotics paper lists benchmarks, reliability/safety, learning-rule adaptability and energy optimisation as open challenges; the field is early and real-world use raises accuracy, adaptability and reliability concerns (Putra et al. 2024) — [arXiv 2404.03325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03325)
- Marchisio & Shafique 2025 survey of neuromorphic computing for autonomous systems names real-time decision-making, continual learning, and secure/resilient systems as open challenges (VERIFIED existence; REPORTED, read: abstract; no evaluation critique in the abstract) — [arXiv 2507.18139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18139)
- LGMD robotics review: little standardised evaluation, mostly qualitative demos, one headline success rate, comparisons with non-bio methods are qualitative (Qin et al. 2025; read: first 100k chars) — [arXiv 2501.02982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02982)
- Mobile-robot neuromorphic navigation review exists (Novo, Lobon, De Marina 2024; VERIFIED existence; abstract read, no evaluation critique in abstract) — [arXiv 2407.06792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06792)
- Interactive-robotics systematic review (Aitsam, Davies, Di Nuovo, IEEE Access 2022, vol 10): existence verified through search results and DOI listing; content not read: NOT VERIFIED — [DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3219440](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3219440)
- A March 2026 framework in Nature Machine Intelligence for evaluating embodied neuromorphic agents beyond task success (response time, energy, adaptability, complexity, environmental impact) was mentioned only in a search-result summary: NOT VERIFIED (I did not open the paper or confirm title/authors) — [CVUT news item](https://aktualne.cvut.cz/en/reports/20260324-how-to-measure-the-intelligence-of-nature-inspired-robots-study-with-first-author)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Common errors/over-interpretations in the field, built from the sources above: (1) energy ratio vs a weak or mismatched baseline and ignoring memory/host cost; (2) reporting max reward or best seed rather than distributions; (3) simulation-only evidence presented as robot capability; (4) no trivial or classical baseline; (5) "biological" label without a control showing the biological structure matters (no source here does a shuffled-structure control for robotics); (6) tuned-on-test; (7) point estimates from few trials.
- INFERRED: Controls that would separate "the fly network decides" from "the designed part decides" (to be developed in the topic 5 report, only listed here as consistent with this literature): strength-preserving and degree-preserving shuffles, mirror/side swap, trivial relay baselines, ablation of the translator (replace the fly network by a same-size random or ANN network with the same I/O), freezing the fly network and re-training only the translator, measuring information that the translator alone can supply (sham input). These are standard ablations in the Huebotter paper style (ablation of components) and the RL-evaluation papers (seeds), but I found no robotics-neuromorphic paper applying them to topology.

### Gaps
- No full-text review of Aitsam 2022 or Davies 2021; their criticisms cannot be quoted.
- No 2025-2026 review specific to neuromorphic manipulation evaluation found; may exist.
