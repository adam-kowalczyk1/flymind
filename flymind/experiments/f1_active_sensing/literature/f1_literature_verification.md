# F1 literature verification and prior-art review

Compiled 2026-11-01. Method: bibliographic metadata (authors, volume, pages, DOI) cross-checked against Crossref; abstracts read from PubMed (NCBI E-utilities) where noted; some items confirmed only from web search snippets (flagged). No DOI, volume or page number below was invented; anything not confirmed is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

Legend for "Evidence type":
- **DIRECT** = the paper tests the same manipulation/effect in a comparable system (none of the papers below is fully direct for F1 - see section D).
- **MECHANISTIC SUPPORT** = shows a mechanism that F1 invokes, in a different system.
- **ANALOGY** = same general idea (active sensing / dither), different system, species, modality, or sensor.

F1 reminder (for mapping): LIF + spike-triggered adaptation (tau_a 300 ms), refractoriness 2.2 ms, hard threshold; connectome-derived LC10a -> ~147 intermediates -> DNa02 L/R; memoryless per-frame chromatic-contrast encoder with pixel threshold and sigmoid azimuth weighting; camera rotated sinusoidally 0.5-4 Hz, 0.5-4 deg; weak stationary target; controls = stationary camera, matched random jitter, P-controller / non-neural detector, degree-preserving shuffles.

## A. The nine references as given

### A1. Biswas et al. (2018) - VERIFIED
**Citation.** Biswas D, Arend LA, Stamper SA, Vagvolgyi BP, Fortune ES, Cowan NJ. Closed-loop control of active sensing movements regulates sensory slip. *Current Biology* 28(24):4029-4036.e4 (2018). DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2018.11.002. PMID 30503617. (Confirmed by Crossref + PubMed + web search; the ambiguity risk is low - the title matches exactly. Note the Cowan lab preprint bioRxiv 10.1101/366609 is the same work.)
**Finding.** Freely swimming *Eigenmannia virescens* shift their fore-aft active-sensing movements when the gain of reafferent feedback is altered (a refuge moved with real-time tracking); a ~4x gain change was compensated so that the magnitude of "sensory slip" stayed roughly constant, which the authors interpret as closed-loop control of the active-sensing movement, separate from the position-keeping controller.
**Supports in F1.** The conceptual framing that sensor movement is a controlled variable, not noise; motivates the "P-controller" comparison and the idea of a controller that regulates *slip* (image motion) rather than maximising it.
**Does NOT support.** That an *oscillation* improves detection of a weak target; that open-loop (pre-programmed) oscillation is beneficial; anything about vision-based L/R detection, spiking nets or thresholds. F1's camera oscillation is open-loop, Biswas is closed-loop.
**Evidence type.** ANALOGY (different species, modality, task, and closed-loop vs. open-loop).
**Limitations.** Behavioural/control-theoretic result; no neural mechanism; no detection-threshold measurement.

### A2. Gomez-Marin et al. (2011) - VERIFIED (but not about vision)
**Citation.** Gomez-Marin A, Stephens GJ, Louis M. Active sampling and decision making in *Drosophila* chemotaxis. *Nature Communications* 2:441 (2011). DOI 10.1038/ncomms1455. PMID 21863008, PMCID PMC3265367.
**Finding.** *Drosophila larvae* orient in odour gradients through runs, stops, lateral head casts and directed turns; negative gradients during runs set the *timing* of turns, positive gradients sampled with high-amplitude head casts set the *direction*. The authors call this active sampling analogous to sniffing.
**Supports in F1.** Only the generic notion that small, deliberate lateral sensor sweeps (head casts) can provide the L/R comparison needed for a directional decision - i.e., sampling by movement.
**Does NOT support.** Anything about adult flies, vision, weak-target detection, dither, adaptation, or oscillatory (periodic) movement - head casts are large, discrete and behaviourally gated, not 0.5-4 deg periodic oscillation.
**Evidence type.** ANALOGY (weak: olfaction, larva).
**Limitations.** Different sensory modality and developmental stage; no neural-circuit model.

### A3. Gammaitoni, Hanggi, Jung, Marchesoni (1998) - VERIFIED
**Citation.** Gammaitoni L, Hanggi P, Jung P, Marchesoni F. Stochastic resonance. *Reviews of Modern Physics* 70(1):223-287 (1998). DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.70.223. (Some indexes give end page 288; Crossref says 223-287.)
**Finding.** Comprehensive review of stochastic resonance (SR): in threshold/bistable systems a non-zero level of noise can maximise the response to a weak (sub-threshold) periodic input; covers theory (two-state model, linear response, SNR), threshold detectors, excitable/neuron models.
**Supports in F1.** The theoretical framing for "threshold dithering": with a hard threshold (pixel threshold and neuronal threshold), a weak signal that never crosses threshold on its own can become detectable when an additional fluctuation is added. Also gives the criteria (SNR/spectral amplification at the forcing frequency) one should use to claim "SR-like".
**Does NOT support.** That a *deterministic, periodic* camera rotation is stochastic resonance. Classical SR needs noise plus a periodic signal; here the weak "signal" is stationary and the periodic term is the dither. Strict SR labelling is therefore not warranted without showing a non-monotonic benefit vs. a noise/jitter amplitude and a matched-noise control. Also see McDonnell & Abbott 2009 (below) on how loosely "SR" is used.
**Evidence type.** MECHANISTIC SUPPORT / analogy (physics theory, not a sensor with sensor motion).
**Limitations.** Review is mostly of 1-D bistable and threshold systems with additive noise; does not treat spike-triggered adaptation or the sensor-motion (image-shift) form of dither.

### A4. Krstic & Wang (2000) - VERIFIED (bibliographic); abstract content partly UNVERIFIED
**Citation.** Krstic M, Wang H-H. Stability of extremum seeking feedback for general nonlinear dynamic systems. *Automatica* 36(4):595-601 (2000). DOI 10.1016/S0005-1098(99)00183-1 (Crossref-confirmed; DOI is case-insensitive).
**Finding (from secondary sources; I could not open the article text).** First rigorous (local) stability proof for perturbation-based extremum seeking: a small sinusoidal perturbation is added to the parameter estimate, the plant output is demodulated/filtered to estimate the gradient, using singular-perturbation + averaging (two time scales). The result is local only; semiglobal-practical results came later (Tan, Nesic, Mareels).
**Supports in F1.** The control-theoretic analogue: a sinusoidal dither whose effect is read out *by demodulation at the dither frequency*. If any F1 readout is phase-locked to the camera rotation (e.g., L/R difference averaged in a window locked to the cycle), that is the extremum-seeking-style gradient estimate. It also supports using a lock-in / matched-filter non-neural baseline as the "classical" comparator.
**Does NOT support.** Any claim about neural benefit; the F1 SNN has no explicit demodulator, and the "P-controller" baseline is not extremum seeking.
**Evidence type.** ANALOGY (control theory), relevant to the comparison design, not to the biology.
**Limitations.** Local stability result; assumes smooth plant maps and time-scale separation, whereas F1 has thresholds, a refractory period, and adaptation tau_a = 300 ms comparable to the dither period at 0.5-4 Hz.

### A5. Viollet (2014) - VERIFIED (single author)
**Citation.** Viollet S. Vibrating makes for better seeing: from the fly's micro-eye movements to hyperacute visual sensors. *Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology* 2:9 (2014). DOI 10.3389/fbioe.2014.00009. PMC4126468. (Review article. Note: it is single-author - "Viollet et al." is a misattribution.)
**Finding.** Review of fly retinal micro-movements and of bio-inspired sensors that mimic them (periodic micro-scanning of a few degrees) to localise contrast edges well below the inter-photoreceptor spacing (hyperacuity). (The "40-fold finer than inter-ommatidial angle" number I saw belongs to Colonnier et al. 2015, not this review - do not attribute it to Viollet 2014.)
**Supports in F1.** Strongest *engineering* precedent that a small, deliberate, periodic oscillation of a sensor improves localisation of a contrast edge - and that the readout is a temporal (phase/amplitude) analysis of the modulated photoreceptor signals.
**Does NOT support.** Benefit for a *weak* (low-contrast / sub-threshold) target; spiking-network readouts; per-frame memoryless thresholding (hyperacute sensors use continuous analog signals and phase information); connectome-derived circuits.
**Evidence type.** ANALOGY, close (same manipulation - sensor vibration - different objective: sub-pixel position, not detection of weak targets).
**Limitations.** Review, not an experiment; measured optical systems with specific photoreceptor angular sensitivity (Gaussian ~ blurred PSF), which a binary-threshold pixel encoder lacks.

### A6. "He et al. (2024)" - AMBIGUOUS in principle; most plausible = Botao He et al., Science Robotics
**Most plausible (VERIFIED).** He B, Wang Z, Zhou Y, Chen J, Singh CD, Li H, Gao Y, Shen S, Wang K, Cao Y, Xu C, Aloimonos Y, Gao F, Fermuller C. Microsaccade-inspired event camera for robotics. *Science Robotics* 9(90):eadj8124 (29 May 2024). DOI 10.1126/scirobotics.adj8124. (arXiv:2405.17769.) Author list from arXiv + Crossref (first 8 authors confirmed by Crossref; remaining authors from arXiv listing).
**Why most plausible.** Exact match to the topic you named (microsaccade-inspired sensor motion, neuromorphic camera, 2024). I did not find another 2024 "He et al." paper on active sensing that fits as well, but I did not exhaustively rule out others (e.g., He et al. in retinal/insect-vision journals) - treat as likely, not certain.
**Finding.** A rotating wedge prism in front of an event-camera lens continually shifts the image, so edges parallel to the camera motion also generate events; the rotation is compensated algorithmically (optics known), giving stable, texture-rich output. Reported advantages in conditions where standard and event cameras fail.
**Supports in F1.** Precedent that *deliberate controlled sensor motion* is used to make a change-detecting sensor responsive to static scene structure - the same logic as "a stationary target is invisible to a change/temporal-contrast detector unless the sensor moves".
**Does NOT support.** Weak-target detection under noise, spiking-network readout, dither benefit vs. matched random jitter (no such control as far as I could read), or any benefit through adaptation. The prism motion is continuous rotation (not small-angle oscillation 0.5-4 deg) - frequency/amplitude were not stated on the arXiv abstract page and were not verified from the full text.
**Evidence type.** ANALOGY, close (neuromorphic + controlled sensor motion); not a test of F1's hypothesis.
**Limitations.** Engineering demonstration; benefit is partly "making static edges visible to an event sensor" - F1's encoder is a per-frame memoryless detector, so the argument that motion is *needed* does not transfer (a memoryless detector already sees a stationary target without motion).

### A7. Stocks (2000) - VERIFIED
**Citation.** Stocks NG. Suprathreshold stochastic resonance in multilevel threshold systems. *Physical Review Letters* 84(11):2310-2313 (2000). DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.84.2310. PMID 11018872.
**Finding.** In arrays of parallel threshold devices with independent noise, the information transmitted about a *suprathreshold* signal is maximised at non-zero noise, extending the dynamic range of the detector beyond the sub-threshold case; the effect is largest when the thresholds adapt to the DC level of the input, which the abstract links to DC adaptation in neurons.
**Supports in F1.** A population of ~147 intermediate neurons with different effective thresholds is the kind of array in which dither/noise can help even for signals that are not strictly sub-threshold; the adaptation remark is conceptually close to F1's spike-triggered adaptation.
**Does NOT support.** A deterministic periodic dither (the paper uses random noise independent per device); a recurrent connectome-structured layer (the model is parallel, uncoupled thresholds); anything about vision.
**Evidence type.** MECHANISTIC SUPPORT (population/threshold level) - analogy for F1's specifics.
**Limitations.** Information-theoretic results with i.i.d. noise and no refractoriness/dynamics.

### A8. Teich (1978) - VERIFIED as most plausible; ambiguous
**Candidates found (all Crossref-verified):**
1. **Teich MC, Matin L, Cantor BI. Refractoriness in the maintained discharge of the cat's retinal ganglion cell. *J Opt Soc Am* 68(3):386-402 (1978). DOI 10.1364/josa.68.000386. PMID 671132.** - MOST PLAUSIBLE: it is neural, it is "refractoriness", and it matches the 2.2-ms refractory parameter in F1.
2. Teich MC, Vannucci G. Observation of dead-time-modified photocounting distributions for modulated laser radiation. *J Opt Soc Am* 68(10):1338 (1978). DOI 10.1364/josa.68.001338 - dead-time in photon counting; same dead-time formalism, no neurons. (Authorship order/co-author spelled "Vannucci" per Crossref; I verified title/DOI, not the full author list.)
**Finding (candidate 1).** The maintained (spontaneous) spike train of cat retinal ganglion cells is not Poisson; it is well described by a dead-time/refractory-modified point process, which reduces interval variability and count variance at higher rates. I could not extract the abstract text (the PDF was a scan and PubMed has no abstract), so the detailed content and the numerical dead-time values are **UNVERIFIED** here; the characterisation above comes from the title, the paper's opening text as shown by a search snippet (refractoriness ~ dead time), and a later paper that cites it.
**Supports in F1.** The legitimacy of including a refractory/dead-time term and the expectation that refractoriness regularises spike counts, which changes the variance (hence detectability, d') of L/R spike-count comparisons.
**Does NOT support.** Any claim about oscillation or adaptation benefits; the ganglion-cell recordings are not fly LC10a/DNa02, and the refractory period for F1 (2.2 ms) is a modelling choice, not from this paper.
**Evidence type.** MECHANISTIC SUPPORT (point-process realism), indirect.
**Limitations.** Retina of the cat, maintained discharge in the dark/steady light; no sensor motion.

### A9. Maimon, Straw, Dickinson (2010) - VERIFIED
**Citation.** Maimon G, Straw AD, Dickinson MH. Active flight increases the gain of visual motion processing in *Drosophila*. *Nature Neuroscience* 13(3):393-399 (2010). DOI 10.1038/nn.2492. PMID 20154683.
**Finding.** In whole-cell recordings from tethered *Drosophila*, the peak-to-peak response of VS (vertical-system) lobula-plate interneurons doubled during flight vs. rest, with reduced membrane resistance suggesting more synaptic drive from upstream motion inputs - the gain of visual processing depends on locomotor state.
**Supports in F1.** The general point that gain of visual pathways in the fly is state/behaviour dependent; that F1's fixed-gain feed-forward LC10a pathway could omit such modulation. (Closer evidence is Hindmarsh Sten et al. 2021: LC10a gain is increased by courtship arousal.)
**Does NOT support.** Anything about self-generated image oscillation, adaptation release, or stochastic resonance. The cells are VS (wide-field motion), not LC10a; the manipulation is behaviour state (flight), not image motion.
**Evidence type.** ANALOGY (state-dependent gain only).
**Limitations.** Tethered, VS cells; different circuit from the F1 target-pursuit pathway.

LC10a matters for F1: both LC10 papers show a *small-moving-object* preference (centre-surround, motion-sensitive). A **stationary** weak target is therefore not the optimal stimulus for LC10a in vivo; camera oscillation would convert a stationary target into a moving one in the retinal frame, which is a *biological* reason to expect a benefit that is independent of any SR mechanism. The F1 encoder is memoryless, so this effect is NOT modelled by the encoder and would only arise via the network's temporal dynamics.

## D. Overall assessment for F1 (my interpretation, not a literature claim)

1. **No paper identified tests F1's hypothesis directly** (periodic small-angle sensor rotation vs. static vs. matched random jitter vs. classical detector, in a connectome-derived SNN with degree-preserving shuffles). Closest precedents: Viollet-lab hyperacute vibrating sensors (sensor oscillation, analog, edge localisation), Testa 2023 / He 2024 (event camera with controlled motion), Rucci 2007 (temporal modulation by FEMs in humans), Martinez-Conde 2013 (FEMs vs. adaptation), Simonotto 1997 (noise helps visual detection), Stocks 2000 / Collins 1995 (threshold arrays + noise). All of these are ANALOGY or MECHANISTIC SUPPORT.
2. **Candidate-mechanism mapping:**
   - *Threshold dithering / SR-like*: supported by SR theory (A3, A7, B7). Caution: deterministic periodic dither with a hard-threshold, memoryless encoder is not "stochastic resonance"; call it "dither-induced threshold crossing" and show a non-monotonic dependence on amplitude and a comparison against matched random jitter before invoking SR (McDonnell & Abbott 2009).
   - *Release from adaptation*: supported conceptually (Martinez-Conde 2013; Benda 2005) but note that a *stationary* target with a memoryless encoder gives constant drive; adaptation would suppress it over ~tau_a, and oscillation re-modulates it. A ~0.5 Hz corner for tau_a = 300 ms (my estimate) lies inside the tested frequency range, so a frequency-dependent effect is plausible and should be tested.
   - *Temporal modulation*: supported by Rucci 2007 (human psychophysics); in F1 it requires a downstream element sensitive to temporal change (adaptation, LC10a motion preference, or demodulation à la Krstić & Wang).
3. **Items that should not be cited as direct support:** Gomez-Marin 2011 (olfactory larva), Maimon 2010 (VS cells in flight), Biswas 2018 (closed-loop, fish), Krstić & Wang 2000 (control theory).
4. **Citation hygiene flags:** Viollet 2014 is single-author; "40-fold" belongs to Colonnier 2015; "He et al. 2024" resolved by topic match, not by an unambiguous reference; "Teich 1978" resolved to Teich-Matin-Cantor (JOSA 68:386); "Caputi" under-specified; Macmillan & Creelman 2nd-edition year uncertain (2004 vs 2005); Rucci 2007 page range differs between PubMed (851-854) and Crossref (852-855).
5. **Not verified**: full text of Krstić & Wang 2000, Teich-Matin-Cantor 1978 (abstract unreadable), He et al. 2024 motion amplitude/frequency, the Rayshubskiy eLife author list/volume, Fenk 2022 abstract, and any statement about whether LC10a connects to DNa02.
